01

Today's AI agents are session-shaped. The physical world is not.

A container spends 45 days in the world, joining a contract PDF to a live event stream to an invoice that lands five weeks later — inside a 30-day dispute window. A chatbot forgets all of it the moment the session ends.

The chatbot

lifespan · ~1 conversation

Wakes, thinks, dies. No memory of the discharge event when the invoice arrives. Cannot sleep for six days, cannot survive a deploy, cannot hold ten thousand containers in mind at once.

attention coverage of a 45-day journey

The durable agent

lifespan · 24–60 days, matches the box

Sleeps for days, wakes on real events, prices its options, acts within a bounded mandate, escalates on spend, and prosecutes the dispute long after the box is gone. The workflow is the memory.

05

Five narrow agents, not one clever one

Narrow agents are easier to prompt, easier to evaluate, easier to constrain — and easier to explain in thirty seconds each. All reasoning lives in activities, never in the deterministic workflow.

01
Watcher
Ingest · Normalise
Turns EDI, portal scrapes, customs codes and contract PDFs into one clean event model. Resolves identity, extracts terms, classifies advisories.
02
Strategist
Predict · Price
Computes the effective LFD, forecasts gate-out, and produces a costed options table with expected values — plus a stored counterfactual.
03
Negotiator
Act on the world
Books drayage, scans for appointment slots continuously, returns empties early, coordinates chassis — all inside a hard spend cap.
04
Auditor
Validate invoice
Runs the 46 CFR Part 541 checklist: issuance deadline, required content, certification clause, tier application, double-billing.
05
Case Builder
Prosecute dispute
Assembles a cited chronology — no citation, no claim — drafts the letter, files inside the window, and runs follow-up for weeks.
06

Seven places the money leaks

The charge is not the leak. The leak is everywhere the organisation fails to prevent, contest, or even notice it — because no single person ever held the contract, the events and the invoice in mind at once.

LEAK
01
2 days

The clock started before you could act

The box was buried in a stack, ungrounded and unappointable. Half the free-time allowance gone to the terminal's own handling — pushing you two days deeper into the tier ladder.

LEAK
02
5 days

Billed for days you were forbidden to move

A CET exam accounts for five billed days. The container was at an examination station being unloaded piece by piece. A penalty that cannot change behaviour is not an incentive.

LEAK
03
$1,225

Billed for the terminal's own capacity failure

Three top-tier days lost to an appointment system with no slots — the strongest claim, most often lost, because it rests on a screenshot nobody took. Evidence perishes faster than the invoice arrives.

LEAK
04
$1,485

Detention on a box the carrier refused to take back

The entire second half of the bill exists because nobody watched the second clock. Return the empty one day earlier and it never happens.

LEAK
05
silent

Nobody knew the deadline until it had passed

Free time in a PDF, discharge in an EDI 315, nothing joining them. Three allowances expired on three different days and not one triggered anything.

LEAK
06
all of it

The dispute window closed before it started

Invoices arrive five weeks late; the team has moved on to 400 other containers. Finance pays anything below a review threshold — regardless of merit or compliance.

LEAK
07
unmeasured

Paid under duress, then never revisited

Cargo release withheld pending settlement. The importer pays to free the goods intending to dispute afterwards — but nothing carries that intent forward, and the protest quietly becomes acceptance.
